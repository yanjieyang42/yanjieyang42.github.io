Read the full abstract

October 2026 · Draft

The expanded 2021 Child Tax Credit raised benefits and paid half of them in monthly advances. Holding each family’s annual credit fixed, we ask what the earlier payments were worth to married and single mothers in the Survey of Income and Program Participation. Relative to comparable women without children, mothers show no detectable change in employment and no detectable relative increase in bank balances; card debt fell, mostly among mothers who held some cash and carried debt, while low cash persisted among single mothers. A monthly consumption-saving model with income and childcare-cost risk, in which some mothers save and others spend all their cash, is estimated by maximum likelihood on each mother’s records with patience set at a standard value. The model-based spender probability averages 31 percent for single mothers and 2 percent for married mothers. Relative to paying the same credit at filing, the 2021 calendar was worth $191 per married mother and $987 per single mother, and $993 and $1,820 for mothers with $1,000 or less in liquid assets. For the average married and single mother, each added credit dollar paid only at filing was worth 92 and 60 cents, against 102 and 96 cents paid as in 2021. The single-mother value is conditional on the spender description: valuing every mother as a saver gives $476, and impatient savers with conventional discount factors, valued at patient rates, give $483 to $539, about half as large.

Working paper

AI-Assisted Personalized Food Recommendations and Diet Quality: Evidence from a Randomized Controlled Trial

With Grace Melo, Rudy Nayga, Azlan Zahid, and Yibin Wang

In a pre-registered randomized trial (AEARCTR-0015970), 839 U.S. adults received either a mobile app delivering AI-personalized meal recommendations or an otherwise identical app delivering general healthy-eating guidance. After seven weeks we find no effect on total diet quality: +0.74 HEI-2020 points (95% CI −3.49 to +4.98). Whole grains, one of thirteen components, is 1.32 points higher under treatment (q = 0.080). Willingness to pay rises $1.29 per standard deviation of perceived relevance (p = 0.001) but is flat in measured dietary change (−$0.15, p = 0.49). Consumers pay for how personalized an app feels, not for what it measurably does.
